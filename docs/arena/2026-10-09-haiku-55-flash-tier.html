🏆 OTC Desk Agent Arena — Claude Haiku 5.5 joins the flash tier

Claude Haiku 5.5 posts the highest card of four flash models, but not by enough to call it a win. Where it is clearly ahead is efficiency: it reaches the answer in 34 tool calls a match, where the others need 40 to 76.

Three of these are the models from our earlier flash-tier board. Haiku 5.5 is the new entrant. All four worked the same six desk days, twice each: a risk-limit breach, an operations and settlement day, a risk-manager control day, a board portfolio review, a trader's RFQ from intake to booking, and a morning of counterparty confirmations read from PDFs and scans.

Before Haiku ran, we checked that it can read an image-only scan (three of three reads of a scanned confirmation returned the underlying, the strike and the full reference correctly), since one of the workflows depends on it.

How it gets there

We read the transcripts of the same 48 trials to see where the saved calls come from. Four measures, per match, averaged over the six workflows:

Measure claude-haiku-5.5 gpt-6-luna mimo-v2.6-flash deepseek-v4.1-flash
Model rounds (calls to the model) 62.9 (1st) 72.4 89.8 89.2
Repeated identical tool calls 8.7 (1st) 10.5 11.2 21.1
Tool calls before the step's expected tool 1.57 (2nd) 2.58 1.49 2.51
Reasoning tokens (thousands) 15.7 (2nd) 13.4 33.1 34.6
  • Fewer rounds. Haiku makes the fewest model calls on five of the six workflows: 13% fewer than luna and about 30% fewer than mimo and deepseek.
  • Fewer wasted calls. It repeats the same call with the same arguments least often. The margin over luna and mimo is small; deepseek repeats a call 2.4 times as often.
  • A quicker route to the right tool. For each step the workflow names the tool it should use. Haiku makes about 1.6 calls before reaching it, against about 2.5 for luna and deepseek. mimo is level with it (1.5).
  • Light reasoning. Haiku spends about as many reasoning tokens as luna, and less than half as many as mimo and deepseek.

Taken together, Haiku reaches the answer in fewer rounds, wastes fewer calls getting there, and does not buy that with extra reasoning. That is a reading of the transcripts, not a tested explanation, and the margins over the second-placed model are small on three of the four measures.

Two things it does not do: it does not use fewer tokens overall (its input is 60% above luna's, because each round carries a larger context), and it does not make fewer errors (about one failed tool call a match, like luna). Its economy is in rounds, not in tokens.

One example: it knows when not to act

On the operations day, the last step asks the agent to reopen a snowball whose knock-out was recorded that morning, while the settlement it generated is still pending. The correct move is to try, be refused by the desk, and report that nothing was recorded. Haiku attempted the reopen, was refused, and reported that it was not recorded, in both trials, and it never voided the pending settlement. luna did so in one of two trials. mimo never refused, and cancelled the knock-out and voided the 512,500 CNY settlement both times. deepseek declined without attempting the call in one trial and reopened in the other.

Where it gives points back

  • It skips skill reads. On risk-manager-control it missed the read-risk-result skill in every trial, and run-risk in most.
  • It overrides a desk adjustment. On ops-settlement, cashflow 9302 was stale because the upstream print had been corrected after ops had manually adjusted the amount to 91,000. The workflow scores keeping the desk's number. In both trials Haiku adopted the re-derived 90,000 instead.
  • Its trader-rfq trials are scattered. Haiku scored 91.1, the lowest of the four on that workflow: it skipped the submit-for-approval step in one trial, and missed an answer and an unsupported-family check in the other.

Caveats

  • Two trials per cell. The top two OVRs are within that noise, and several models' two trials on one workflow differ by 8 to 14 points (luna scored 100 and 85.7 on high-board).
  • One workflow was run twice for every model, and this board uses the second pair. On the first pair Haiku is second, 0.1 behind mimo. That is why we call the top a tie.
  • One effort level. Every contestant ran at high. Haiku's other levels are unmeasured, and the gateway's acceptance of them says nothing about whether they differ.
  • EFF is relative to each workflow's par, and counts tool calls; rounds and tokens are reported separately above. A model can be efficient in calls and still spend heavily per call, as Haiku's cost shows.
  • Latency is not reported. Wall-clock time depends on the gateway as much as the model.
