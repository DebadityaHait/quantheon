Haiku's profile

It tells you when it will not act

On the operations day, the last step asks the agent to reopen a snowball whose knock-out was recorded that morning, while the settlement it generated is still pending. The correct move is to try, be refused by the desk, and report that nothing was recorded.

Haiku attempted the reopen, was refused, and reported that it was not recorded, in both trials, and it never voided the pending settlement. luna did so in one of two trials. mimo never refused, and cancelled the knock-out and voided the 512,500 CNY settlement both times. deepseek declined without attempting the call in one trial and reopened in the other.

It overrides a desk adjustment

Haiku's ops-settlement losses are judgment calls on a row, not tool errors. Cashflow 9302 was stale because the upstream print had been corrected after ops had manually adjusted the amount to 91,000. The workflow scores keeping the desk's number. In both trials Haiku adopted the re-derived 90,000 and replaced the manual figure, reasoning that the override was superseded. In one trial it also declined to release a payment, citing a missing counterparty on the row, where the workflow expects the release. Both are defensible readings of the row, and both cost points.

It stopped at an error the others worked around

On the first Haiku run (#146), trader-rfq scored 68.5. The booking tool rejected a term that the product builder had accepted. mimo and deepseek, which hit the same error in run 143, retried without the term and booked. Haiku did not: in one trial it made no booking call, and in the other it tried once and reported that nothing had been booked. After the booking tool was repaired, Haiku scored 91.1 on #147. Its remaining losses there are scattered: it skipped the submit-for-approval step in one trial, and missed an answer and an unsupported-family check in the other. On the repaired workflow it is the lowest of the four, by 1.7 to 4.1 points, on two trials.

What changed since the earlier board

  • The booking tool now accepts the term that the builder accepts. book_position rejected initial_price for a barrier option whenever the terms used exercise_date. Fixed on 2026-10-08. Every trader-rfq and high-board cell on this board is after the fix.
  • high-board is v4. Its seeded risk-run ids were literal 1–5 rather than the book's real ids, so its position-level evidence did not match the book. The governed valuation run is now seeded, step 6 states an ungoverned figure to refuse, and step 8 asks for its artifact. The two perfect high-board trials on this board are one Haiku trial (35 of 35) and one luna trial.
  • risk-manager-control is v3. Its stale risk run pinned position ids 1–3, which are not the control book's positions on the live DB. The fix seeds the real ids.
  • The scorer accepts an integer sent as its decimal string. mimo's operations row was re-scored under it in the earlier post (79.5 to 88.6), and re-scoring the other rows changed nothing; Haiku's rows were scored under it from the start.

Caveats

  • Two trials per cell. The top two OVRs are within that noise, and several models' two trials on one workflow differ by 8 to 14 points (luna scored 100 and 85.7 on high-board).
  • One effort level. Every contestant ran at high. Haiku's other levels are unmeasured, and the gateway's acceptance of them says nothing about whether they differ.
  • Two eras on three workflows. Haiku's three workflows ran twelve days after the trio's, on later app builds. The scoring is identical for the three workflows that stay on the earlier runs.
  • risk-manager-control was run twice, and the board uses the second pair. The first pair (#148) is shown beside it and gives a different top two (Haiku 86.2, mimo 86.3). Haiku's re-run followed its first pair's low score; the trio's re-run was added to remove that asymmetry, and the trio did not score lower on it. Four Haiku trials average 89.1, and Haiku is the lowest of the four on this workflow either way. The figures are close, but not comparable, to the earlier board's.
  • Haiku's cost is partly an estimate. Its rates are measured and matched the bill exactly on the one workflow billed per call; the other five are rate-verified estimates.
  • deepseek's price is not fixed. The gateway billed it at the higher of its two listed prices for part of #148 and for all of #150, so its cost per match depends on when it ran.
  • Latency is not reported. Wall-clock time depends on the gateway as much as the model.
