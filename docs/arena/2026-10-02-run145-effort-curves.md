## The question

Run #143 put three flash models through the desk's six workflows at `high`
reasoning effort. This board asks what the effort setting itself buys. Each model
got the levels its own route really offers:

| Model | Arms run here | Re-used | Points on its curve |
|---|---|---|---|
| **gpt-6-luna** | `low`, `medium`, `xhigh` | `high` | 4 |
| **mimo-v2.6-flash** | `low`, `medium` | `high` | 3 (its ladder stops at `high`) |
| **deepseek-v4.1-flash** | `low`, `max` | `high` | 3 (its real ladder) |

three: `low`, `high` and `max`. The other four are aliases that run one of those
three, so we ran only the real ones. Arms of the same workflow ran back to back, so
the comparisons that matter are within a workflow.

## Luna's peak is a handful of coin flips

Luna's mean jumps from 90.6 at `medium` to 96.4 at `high` and falls back to 92.6
at `xhigh`. Before reading that as "Luna is best at `high`", we tallied every check
across its arms. Of 250 checks, `high` differs from the other arms' average on
**15**, and nearly all of them are on two workflows:

- **High-board review (+5.3 net):** whether the model reuses the stored governance
  report, and whether it writes the board report as an artifact. These steps flip
  between trials in *every* arm. The artifact step passed at `low` 2/2,
  `medium` 0/2, `high` 2/2 and `xhigh` 1/2.
- **Trader RFQ (+1.8 net):** the order of the draft and quote calls, and whether it
  submits for approval. Luna's `low` trials here scored 93.5 and 56.5 on the same
  inputs.

Luna's trial-to-trial spread is 8.6 points, the largest of the three models. Its
`xhigh` arm scored 68.6 and 91.4 on high-board. The `high` arm happens to have won
these coin flips twice each, in a different week. We report its curve as
**unresolved**, not peaked.

## Does each workflow have its own best effort?

A common claim, and the one vendors' guidance leans on, is that the right effort
depends on the task. We tested it on this board and on our earlier Luna study,
Run #110 (gpt-5.6-luna, `none`/`low`/`high`/`max`, five workflows, two trials).

A table of per-workflow winners always looks task-specific. With two trials per
cell, the top cell in each column is partly noise. So we asked two sharper
questions.

**1. Does the effect of effort differ by workflow more than noise does?** This is
an effort × workflow interaction. We tested it by permuting the residuals of an
additive model (20,000 permutations), so nothing assumes the scores are
normally distributed.

**2. Would choosing effort per workflow beat one setting for everything?** We chose
each workflow's best effort from trial A and scored the choice on trial B, then
swapped. The alternative chooses one effort for all workflows the same way.

| Model | Interaction p (objective / OVR) | Per-task pick vs one setting, on held-out trials (objective / OVR) |
|---|:--:|:--:|
| deepseek-v4.1-flash | 0.35 / 0.22 | +1.5 (p 0.05) / 0.0 |
| gpt-6-luna | 0.85 / 0.98 | −0.9 / −0.1 |
| mimo-v2.6-flash | 0.79 / 0.52 | +0.7 / +0.8 |
| gpt-5.6-luna (Run #110) | 0.23 / 0.15 | −0.3 / −3.6 |

*#145 arms only; adding the re-used `high` point changes none of the conclusions
(interaction p 0.11–0.96).*

- **No interaction is significant**, for any model, on either measure.
- **Choosing per workflow does not pay on held-out trials.** For both Luna studies
  it is *worse* than one global setting, because the per-workflow winners were
  mostly noise. Picking with hindsight on the *same* trial looks much better
  (gpt-6-luna 96 against 88). That gap is the illusion a per-workflow table
  creates. DeepSeek's +1.5 is borderline and vanishes once cost counts (OVR 0.0).
- **What this cannot rule out.** Two trials per cell is little power. A simulation
  at our measured noise shows an 80% chance of detecting a single workflow that
  gains **17 points** (DeepSeek), 26 (MiMo, gpt-5.6-luna) or **41** (gpt-6-luna)
  at a higher effort. Smaller task-specific effects may exist. Within this desk's
  family of workflows, none is large enough to see, or to act on with a desk's
  realistic budget of trials.

This also corrects our own earlier reading of Run #110. That report said effort's
sign is "task-shaped", from per-workflow differences such as high-board gaining
18.6 points at `max`. Under the interaction test (p 0.23) and on held-out trials
(−0.3), those differences are within noise.

### What the literature says

The claim has real support where tasks differ in **difficulty or kind**:

- [Snell et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314) find the compute-optimal
  test-time budget depends on prompt difficulty.
- [Wu et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07266) find accuracy rises then
  falls with chain-of-thought length, with the best length growing with difficulty.
- [Gema et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14417) find longer reasoning
  *lowers* accuracy on some task types.
- Anthropic's effort documentation says the impact of effort "varies by task type".
  OpenAI and Google give similar guidance.

The evidence from **agents** is thinner and mostly recent preprints. It points to the
pattern we see:

- [Cuadron et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08235) find agents that
  overthink resolve fewer software issues.
- [Ares (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07915) chooses effort per agent *step*
  and saves up to 53% of reasoning tokens at little loss.
- [Li et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18998) find neither more
  sequential nor more parallel compute reliably helps general agents.

The dominant effect is cost rising while correctness stays flat. DeepSeek's own
documentation lists the same collapse of seven effort names into three that we
found on its route.

**Our reading:** the best effort varies between *kinds* of task, such as math
against retrieval. Within one family, here six agentic desk workflows, one cheap
setting serves every workflow about as well as tuning each one, at a fraction of
the cost.

## Methods and caveats

- **Harness defects found during this board, fixed before it finished.** The
  orchestrator was running two summarizers. The deepagents 0.7 upgrade had
  appended our compaction middleware instead of replacing the default, so after
  a compaction the model saw only part of its own history. Separately, a malformed
  tool call with an id left an unanswered tool result behind. Both broke DeepSeek
  requests with a 400. Every affected arm (DeepSeek `max` on three workflows, Luna
  `medium` on one) was set aside with its evidence and re-run on the fixed code;
  none of their earlier scores appear here. After the fix, 226 compacted model calls
  ran with no failed request.
- **The re-used `high` point** ran a week earlier (2026-09-26/27). Since then the
  only scoring change is the integer-as-string argument fix, already applied to
  those rows, and every workflow manifest is unchanged. The difference in time is
  the remaining confound, and it is why Luna's curve is unresolved.
- **One cell has a single trial:** MiMo `medium` on trader RFQ lost its second
  trial to a network error, excluded by the plan's rules.
- **Cost is list price** at the low end of each route's range. On #143/#144 the
  billed totals matched it almost to the cent (Luna $0.581, MiMo $0.895, DeepSeek
  $0.588), so we expect the same here; billed figures will replace these.
- **Two trials per cell** is the board's limit, and the power section above is the
  honest statement of what that can and cannot show.
